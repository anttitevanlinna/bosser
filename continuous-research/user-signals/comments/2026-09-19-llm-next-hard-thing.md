# Signal: When LLMs make problem-solving cheap, the hard thing moves back to business fundamentals

**Insight in one sentence:** Once LLMs can conjure a plausible solution to any (digital) business problem, everyone has solutions — so the market saturates on "solved," and competitive advantage shifts back to the classic fundamentals LLMs cannot supply: distinctiveness and precision.

## The setup

- LLMs can create anything and everything, especially digital stuff. Any idea, a solution to any problem.
- That means everybody will have a lot of solutions. Abundance of solutions = saturation of the market.
- So we come back to the fundamentals of what makes a great business.

## Axis 1: Distinctiveness (curation)

- Differentiation, choosing your battles, being yourself.
- Curating: choosing the field where we will be the absolute best.
- Back to Playing to Win, Porter-style differentiation.
- Why the LLM can't do this: **the LLM gives you averages.** Many plausible things. Without grounding in customers it cannot determine what is optimal vs. not — it can guess, but it's a guess.

## Axis 2: Precision

- Doing *precisely* right for the chosen customer, in the chosen situation, on the chosen job-to-be-done.
- The precision of rightness is again something the LLM cannot figure out on its own — however hard you hypothetically ask it to "do the right thing."
